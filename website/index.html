02 / THE RESEARCH QUESTION

A good edit starts with the right source.

A successful edit changes the intended source and preserves the rest. However, existing editors can miss the target, edit the wrong source, or change both. We find that source-selection errors emerge early in denoising and often persist through later steps. Short corrective prefixes can rescue failed edits, while corruptive prefixes can induce failures, showing that early trajectories shape the final source choice.

These findings motivate STEER, a two-stage post-training framework. STEER-Emphasis strengthens training on task-critical early states to reduce source-selection errors. STEER-Recovery learns corrective denoising from the model’s own early rollout states to recover from remaining deviations. Applying both stages to our pretrained six-task editor yields SteerSound, combining broad editing capability with more reliable source selection.

Read the abstract +

Unified audio editors support diverse operations, yet often struggle to modify the intended source while preserving non-target content. We show that source selection in audio editing is shaped by early denoising states, while incorrect early selections can hardly be corrected during subsequent denoising.

Motivated by these findings, we propose Source-selection Training via Early-trajectory Emphasis and Recovery (STEER), a two-stage post-training algorithm for reliable selective audio editing. STEER-Emphasis prioritizes training on task-critical early states to reduce source-selection errors, while STEER-Recovery learns corrective denoising from self-generated rollout states to recover from early deviations.

To systematically assess source-selection reliability, we introduce the Selective Audio Editing Benchmark, enabling large-scale automatic evaluation of target manipulation and non-target preservation. Applying STEER to our pretrained multitask base editor gives SteerSound, a unified audio editor with leading selective editing performance among open-source audio editors. Experiments on our base editor, UNISON, and SAM Audio demonstrate that STEER consistently improves both selective editing capability and overall editing performance. Early-trajectory emphasis further brings substantial improvements in transcript-conditioned target speaker removal and separation, highlighting the broader applicability of this training principle.

Observation: source-selection errors emerge early +

We decode estimated clean audio from intermediate denoising states to inspect which source the model is tending to edit. In representative failed edits from UNISON and SAM Audio, incorrect source-selection tendencies are already perceptible within the first 1–5 of 50 denoising steps.

These observations locate when an error becomes visible. The interventions below test how changing early states affects the final edit.

Explore the evidence: corrective and corruptive prefix interventions +

Do early states influence which source is ultimately edited? We test this with two interventions on UNISON and SAM Audio.

Corrective: rescue a failed edit

Replace the first k predicted denoising velocities with ones derived from the correct editing target.

Corruptive: disrupt a successful edit

Replace the first k predicted denoising velocities with ones derived from a target encoding incorrect source selection.

After either intervention, the model resumes its own denoising under the original instruction. We evaluate 100 validation examples per task.

Short correct prefixes can rescue failed edits, while short incorrect prefixes can induce failures. Extending the interventions into later steps adds limited effects. These findings motivate STEER-Emphasis to prevent early selection errors and STEER-Recovery to correct remaining deviations.

03 / THE METHOD

Reliable source selection with STEER.

STEER-Emphasis reduces early source-selection errors. STEER-Recovery learns to recover from remaining trajectory deviations.

Pretrained base editor→01 · STEER-Emphasis→02 · STEER-Recovery→SteerSound

01 · STEER-Emphasis

Emphasize task‑critical early states.

STEER-Emphasis focuses training on the high-noise states critical to source selection. Corrective prefix interventions identify the early range for each task.

Training details

For selective tasks, noise levels are sampled uniformly within these ranges, using the standard Flow Matching objective.

02 · STEER-Recovery

Recover from early trajectory deviations.

STEER-Recovery trains on the model’s own early rollout states, learning corrective denoising toward the desired clean edit.

Training details

Rollout depth increases progressively, and the best validation checkpoint is retained. Generate and Add use standard Flow Matching.

Corrective target = z(k) − z0σk
Base editor architecture 673M trainable backbone · 1.348B total parameters+

A unified, dual-branch audio editor.

A SeedEdit-inspired DiT lets reference and generated audio exchange information through multimodal self-attention with shared attention parameters. It combines a 673.35M-parameter trainable backbone, T5-Base, an audio VAE, and a HiFi-GAN vocoder.

The same editor supports generation, addition, removal, replacement, pitch changes, and volume changes.
