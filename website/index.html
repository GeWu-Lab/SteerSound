02 / THE RESEARCH QUESTION

A good edit starts with the right source.

A successful edit changes the intended source and preserves the rest. Existing editors may miss the target, edit the wrong source, or change both. Our interventions show that early denoising states shape the final source choice: correcting an early trajectory can rescue a failed edit, while corrupting it can induce failure. This motivates STEER: Emphasis focuses training on critical early states to reduce selection errors, and Recovery learns to correct deviations from the model’s own trajectories.

Explore the evidence: corrective and corruptive prefix interventions +

For failed edits, correct early prefixes can rescue the outcome. For successful edits, deliberately corrupted prefixes can induce failures. The model then resumes its own denoising under the original instruction. Interventions use 100 validation examples per task; the critical ranges are identified on validation data.

Read the abstract +

Unified audio editors support diverse operations, yet often struggle to modify the intended source while preserving non-target content. To strengthen this selective editing capability, we propose Source-selection Training via Early-trajectory Emphasis and Recovery (STEER), a two-stage post-training framework, and use it to develop SteerSound, a unified audio editing model supporting six common tasks.

Intermediate-state observation and bidirectional prefix interventions show that early denoising states causally influence final source selection, while incorrect early decisions often persist through subsequent denoising. Guided by these findings, STEER-Emphasis emphasizes training on task-relevant early-trajectory states to reduce source-selection errors, while STEER-Recovery learns corrective denoising from self-generated rollout states to recover from early-trajectory deviations.

We further construct audio editing data in two complementary settings. The Selective setting enables large-scale automatic evaluation of target manipulation and non-target preservation, while the Complex setting contains richer multi-source scenes. Experiments in both settings show that STEER consistently improves selective editing reliability, reducing source-selection errors while improving overall editing performance. Further evaluations across different models and other selective-control tasks also support the broad generalizability of STEER and its underlying early-trajectory training rationale. SteerSound achieves advanced performance among open-source unified editors.

The editor beneath STEER 673M trainable backbone · 1.348B total parameters+

A unified, dual-branch audio editor.

A SeedEdit-inspired DiT lets reference and generated audio exchange information through multimodal self-attention with shared attention parameters. It combines a 673.35M-parameter trainable backbone, T5-Base, an audio VAE, and a HiFi-GAN vocoder.

The same editor supports generation, addition, removal, replacement, pitch changes up to ±4 semitones, and volume changes up to ±5 dB.
